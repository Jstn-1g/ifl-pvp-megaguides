## How Marvel Rivals Roles Work

Marvel Rivals organizes heroes into three official classes: **Vanguard, Duelist, and Strategist**. The developer's hero-design notes describe role assignment as part of translating each character's powers and personality into a distinct playable kit.

### Vanguard

Vanguards create and hold space. A useful Vanguard evaluation cannot stop at raw survivability; it must also consider initiation, peel, sightline control, objective presence, and how reliably the hero enables the rest of a composition.

The Vanguard tier table therefore scores:

- space creation and objective control
- initiation and disengage reliability
- protection for Strategists and vulnerable Duelists
- map and composition dependence
- Team-Up value in the current patch

### Duelist

Duelists convert space and openings into pressure, eliminations, or forced cooldowns. Damage totals alone can be misleading, so the rankings separate reliable pressure from burst that depends on narrow setups.

The Duelist tier table scores:

- reliable damage and finishing power
- mobility, angles, and self-sufficiency
- ability to pressure Vanguards and backlines
- mechanical burden relative to payoff
- current Team-Up and composition fit

### Strategist

Strategists sustain, enable, and stabilize a team. Season 9 also demonstrates why the class needs patch-aware analysis: the July 11 emergency post changed healing-to-energy conversion for every Strategist only one day after the season update.

The Strategist tier table scores:

- healing or mitigation reliability
- utility, saves, and Ultimate impact
- survivability under dive pressure
- synergy with common compositions
- performance after the current energy-economy changes

## Season 9 Tier List Methodology

The guide publishes **three role-separated tier lists**, not one combined ranking. Comparing a Vanguard directly with a Strategist produces a clean-looking table but weak advice; the roles solve different problems and should be judged against their peers.

Each placement uses four labels:

1. **Patch:** the exact balance baseline.
2. **Evidence:** official change history plus clearly labeled competitive or community evidence where needed.
3. **Confidence:** high, medium, or provisional.
4. **Reason:** a concise explanation tied to the scoring criteria above.

Tier definitions remain stable even when placements move:

| Tier | Meaning |
| --- | --- |
| S | Consistently shapes strong compositions or answers the patch's central problems with limited tradeoffs. |
| A | Strong and broadly useful, with manageable weaknesses or slightly greater composition dependence. |
| B | Viable when drafted for the right map, matchup, or player strength, but not a default answer. |
| C | Narrow, inconsistent, or clearly outperformed in the current patch; still playable with a specific plan. |
| Provisional | Insufficient current evidence after a new hero, rework, or system change. |

For this first Season 9 read, Counterwatch's July 15 all-ranks table supplies the primary community match signal through sample-adjusted win rates. RivalsMeta's Diamond+ table is a directional cross-check for heroes whose performance changes sharply with coordination or mechanical skill. Official patch notes remain the source of truth for what changed; neither community tracker is treated as an official balance source.

Because the season is young, every placement below has **medium confidence**. A hero can move quickly as players learn the Team-Up system, and a strong win rate does not prove that a hero is the best choice on every map or in every composition.

## Jubilee Quick Guide

Jubilee is a Strategist who mixes projectile healing, enemy pressure, speed, crowd control, and a large area Ultimate. Her kit rewards alternating between ally support and offensive marks instead of treating healing and damage as separate modes.

| Ability | Practical job at the 20260711 baseline |
| --- | --- |
| Energy Plasmoids | Rapid light blasts heal allies and damage enemies. Triggering Sparkle Marks temporarily increases attack speed and lets Jubilee fire without spending energy. |
| Blooming Ball | A tracking vampiric orb damages enemies and heals allies around it. Striking the orb or nearby targets charges it, increasing its size and healing; the hotfix reduced the required charge to 250 and raised base healing to 35 per second. |
| Sparking Sprint | A mobility and tempo tool that boosts speed, jump height, and attack speed while allowing free Energy Plasmoid fire. Use it to rotate, escape pressure, or compress a burst-healing window. |
| Dazzling Detonation | An enemy-facing detonation deals damage and blinds; transferring the energy to an ally provides healing and speed instead. It is Jubilee's clearest moment-to-moment choice between disruption and rescue. |
| Sparkle Mark | Mark detonations provide allies with healing amplification and Bonus Health, or deal burst damage to enemies. The mark system connects the rest of the kit. |
| Firework Finale | Jubilee knocks back nearby enemies and surrounds herself with orbiting fireworks that heal allies and damage enemies. The July 11 hotfix reduced its energy cost to 4,300. |

Her most important early review question is not raw healing output; it is whether she can safely keep marks and Blooming Ball active while saving Dazzling Detonation for the fight's critical swing. The Blade Team-Up increases Blooming Ball's Life Steal ratio to 30%, so any Jubilee placement must state whether that pairing was assumed.

## Team-Up System

Team-Ups are one of Marvel Rivals' defining draft layers, but they are also one of the most volatile parts of the guide. Season 9 replaced the previous structure with a ground-up revamp, and the July 11 emergency post explicitly referenced the removal of Team-Up Anchors. The launch balance notes also removed the former flat Anchor bonuses from numerous heroes and repeatedly describe the new choice as selecting a **Team-Up partner**.

Official hero profiles now separate a Team-Up ability into a **Base Effect** and an **Enhanced Effect**. The base effect tells you what the hero gains from selecting that ability; the enhanced effect tells you what changes when the named partner is in the composition. That makes the drafting question more precise than “does this pair have a Team-Up?”: players need to compare the base tool, the partner-only upgrade, and the opportunity cost of the other available option.

| Strategist damage | 55% | 55% |
| Strategist healing | 60% | 70% |

The practical result is a slower damage-fed Ultimate cycle for every role than the previous 70% or 75% baselines, with Strategists recovering some—but not all—of their former healing-fed rate. Team planning should therefore track actual Ultimate readiness instead of relying on Season 8 timing instincts.

### Regenerative Shields

Season 9 introduced Regenerative Shields as a new health type. They interact with percentage-based healing and damage in the same way as base Health. Their unique rule is timing-based: after the owner avoids damage for five seconds, the chargeable shield pool regenerates completely over the following five seconds.

The launch balance post gave Regenerative Shields to Angela, Captain America, Hulk, Rogue, Thor, Venom, Black Cat, Black Panther, Daredevil, Magik, Psylocke, and Star-Lord. That creates two clear fight-management rules:

- If you are attacking one of those heroes, light but reliable follow-up damage can deny the five-second regeneration trigger and stop a disengage from becoming a free reset.
- If you are playing one of them, breaking line of sight is valuable only if you stay safe long enough to start—and then finish—the regeneration window. Five seconds without damage only starts recovery; ten uninterrupted seconds from the last hit are needed to restore the complete chargeable pool.

Because the shield replaces base Health on several heroes rather than simply adding durability, old total-health comparisons can be misleading. Evaluate effective health together with how consistently the hero can leave pressure and earn the regeneration window.

### Hero Swapping and Composition Repair

Treat swapping as a decision workflow: identify whether the team lacks space, finishing power, sustain, peel, or a specific counter; then change the smallest number of picks needed to solve that problem.

| Symptom | First question | Lowest-disruption repair |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Team cannot cross an open lane | Is the frontline creating a safe timing or merely absorbing damage? | Change the Vanguard plan or add a route-denial tool before replacing the entire damage line. |
| Targets survive every engage | Is pressure spread across too many enemies? | Add reliable finishing or focus-fire utility; do not assume another high-score damage pick solves coordination. |
| Strategists die first | Is the threat dive, range, or a lost sightline? | Add peel, shorten the backline's rotation, or deny the attack route. |
| Team wins space but loses the objective | Are players chasing beyond their sustain and cover? | Keep one stable objective presence and shorten pursuit distance. |
| A planned Team-Up is not producing value | Is the enhanced effect actually solving the current fight? | Switch the Team-Up choice or one partner, rather than preserving a synergy that exists only on paper. |
| Ultimates arrive too late | Is the team using outdated pre-Season 9 timing expectations? | Track the current 55% damage and 70% Strategist-healing conversion baselines and plan one fight ahead. |

### Destructible Terrain and Sightlines

Map geometry affects routes, cover, firing angles, and the safety of backlines. This guide distinguishes durable positioning principles from route-specific advice that needs its own patch or map check.
